What should I record before anyone visits

Begin with what you can see. Mark the likely pool zone, where ground steps or falls, existing walls, fences, sheds, downpipes, damp patches and the tightest access points. Photograph the street frontage, boundary and probable work area so scale is easier to explain later.

Keep two notebooks or lists. One holds observed facts such as access limits, visible drainage paths, levels and nearby structures. The other holds matters that need professional confirmation, including approvals, certification and engineering questions tied to the wall, soil behaviour, pool shell and proposed work. Blurring those lists tends to create quotes with hidden allowances.

  • Intended pool position and desired finished level.
  • Where retained ground begins, turns or meets existing structures.
  • How an excavator, crane or materials could realistically reach the area.
  • Water flow after rain, roof runoff, garden drainage and pooling near the shell.
  • Reports already held, plus photos if a provider requests them.

Which questions stay open until advice is checked

Some items should not be treated as settled by a website, a neighbour or an early estimate. Approval, certification and engineering requirements remain project specific. For that reason, ask what depends on the exact wall, pool position and nearby structure before comparing dollar figures.

Useful open questions include: does the proposal raise an engineering issue; what details are needed before approval requirements can be discussed; how could machinery movement change the sequence; where should drainage be observed before excavation or paving conceals it; and what should be listed as included, excluded or provisional so allowances are not mistaken for fixed scope.

For renewal work, slow the comparison down. Ask what can be inspected first, such as wall condition, footing behaviour, drainage behind the wall and movement already visible. For new work, keep decisions open until levels, access, drainage and property specific requirements are verified.

How do new work and renewal differ

New construction and renewal should not be forced into the same template. A new pool near a change in level may need integrated planning across shell, retaining, drainage, waterproofing and surrounds. The planning prompts worth naming in one brief are integrated pool and retaining planning, sloping site pool excavation planning, structural retaining wall coordination, pool drainage and waterproofing coordination, and concrete shell plus surround planning.

Renewal starts from evidence already on site. Before choosing repair, partial rebuild or integrated replacement, ask what can be reviewed: the existing footing, wall condition, drainage path, proximity to the pool and any visible movement. That review can shape whether the job is minor, staged or more connected than it first appears.

What should a quote request make visible

A stronger request shows assumptions rather than hiding them. State the intended pool location, desired finished level, retained ground limits, access notes, drainage observations, nearby structures and interfaces between coping, paving, fences, barriers and the dig zone. Attach photos and reports if asked.
